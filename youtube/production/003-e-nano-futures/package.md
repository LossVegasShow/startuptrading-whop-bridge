*[Cold open: three contract cards appear side by side. Under each, the dollars per point type on: $50, $5, $0.50.]*

> Fifty dollars a point. Five dollars a point. Fifty cents a point. In August, CME launched a futures contract one-hundredth the size of the E-mini. By the end of this you'll know exactly who it's for, and there's one cost that makes it a bad deal for some people, which I'll get to.

*[Cut to Jack.]*

> Three rounds: what it is, the same trade in all three sizes, and who should use which.
>
> **What an E-nano is.** It's the same S&P 500, the same Nasdaq, just smaller. An E-mini S&P is $50 a point. The micro is a tenth of that, $5. The E-nano is a hundredth, 50 cents a point. On the Nasdaq it's the same idea: $20, $2, 20 cents. *[Jack: confirm tick sizes and where it trades from CME's specs before recording, and show the NinjaTrader contract page.]*

*[Chart: ES, a 6-point stop drawn. The Risk Card appears three times, one per contract.]*

> **Same trade, three sizes.** Say I'm willing to risk $100 on this trade, and the stop is 6 points. One ES at 6 points is $300. That's three times my limit, so the math says zero. One MES is $30, so I can take 3, that's $90. The E-nano is $3 a contract. $100 divided by 3 is 33.3, so 33 E-nanos, $99 of risk.
>
> Notice what just happened. With a hundred dollars of risk, the E-nano is the only one that lets me get to almost exactly my number. That's the point of it. It's a precision tool.

*[Cut to Jack. On screen: "The catch".]*

> **The cost nobody mentions.** Here's the loop I opened at the start. Commissions are charged per contract, and they don't shrink a hundred times when the contract does. Say your broker charges 25 cents a side per E-nano. *[Jack: replace with a real current fee from his broker.]* 33 contracts in and out is about $16.50. That's a sixth of your whole risk, gone before the trade does anything.
>
> So the E-nano is precise, but on small risk amounts the fees can eat a real slice. Always run the fee math next to the size math.

*[Chart: the three Risk Cards again, with fees added.]*

> **Who each one fits.** If your risk per trade is a few hundred dollars or more, the micro usually fits, and the fees are a small part of it. If your risk is very small, or you want to add size gradually, the E-nano gives you control, as long as you've checked the fees. The E-mini is for when your risk comfortably covers a full contract on a normal stop.

*[Jack to camera.]*

> **The verdict.** The E-nano isn't a beginner toy and it isn't a gimmick. It's the smallest step size futures have ever had. Use it when the math says zero on the micro, and check the fees first.
>
> What size are you trading right now? Tell me in the comments. The calculator I used is free, link's in the description. Next, the full comparison, ES vs MES vs E-nano, on screen now.
