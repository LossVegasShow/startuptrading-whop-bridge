# Why the Same Stop Risks Twice as Much on CPI Day

**When:** Publish the day of the next CPI release, after the session. Check the BLS release calendar for the date and fill in the day's real numbers.

**Structure:** The Breakdown · **Length:** about 9 minutes · **Chapters:** 0:00 The bar · 0:40 The plan before · 2:00 8:30 · 3:30 What ATR did · 5:30 What the size became · 7:30 The rule

*[Cold open: ES 5-minute chart. The 8:30 ET CPI bar prints, much larger than the bars before it. Freeze.]*

> Look at the size of that bar. Same market as yesterday, same setup I'd normally take, and if I'd used yesterday's stop, it would have been gone in seconds. Here's what CPI morning does to your risk, and why the right size today was smaller than you'd think.

*[Cut to Jack.]*

> Three parts: the plan I had before the number, what the release did to volatility, and what that did to my size.
>
> **The plan before.** *[Jack: the real pre-market plan from that CPI day, with the Risk Card.]* On a normal morning, ATR on the 5-minute is around [4] points, so my stop's about one and a half ATRs, [6] points. On ES that's [$300] a contract.
>
> **8:30.** *[The release on the chart.]* The number comes out and the market moves more in five minutes than it did in the previous hour. *[Jack: what he did in the first minutes, which is usually nothing.]*

*[ATR readout on screen, before and after.]*

> **What ATR did.** After the release, ATR on the 5-minute jumps to around [9] points. So a stop that respects the market is now one and a half times 9, about [13.5] points.
>
> Here's the trap. If you keep yesterday's 6-point stop, the dollars at risk look the same, $300 a contract. But measured against how far the market is moving, that stop is now less than half as wide as it should be. It gets hit by noise. That's the sense in which the same stop risks twice as much: twice as likely to be taken out by a move that means nothing.

*[The calculator on screen.]*

> **What the size became.** So I widen the stop to [13.5] points to respect the market, and my risk stays at [$500]. One ES at 13.5 points is [$675]. Over my limit, so the math says zero ES. On MES it's [$67.50] a contract, so [7] micros, [$472.50] of risk. Same dollars as a normal day, a wider stop, fewer contracts.
>
> *[Jack: whether he traded that morning at all. "Sometimes the answer is to sit out the first 15 minutes" is a valid ending.]*

*[Jack to camera.]*

> **The rule.** On news days, let ATR widen the stop, and let the wider stop shrink the size. Never keep the normal stop and hope. And it's fine if the math says zero.
