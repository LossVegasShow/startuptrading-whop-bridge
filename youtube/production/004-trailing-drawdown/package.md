## 3. Full script (read once, then talk it in his own words)

**Target search:** trailing drawdown explained · **Pillar:** Prop-firm · **Chapters:** 0:00 The line that follows you · 1:30 How it trails · 4:00 Why good traders fail · 6:00 Working backwards · 8:30 A daily stop · 10:00 Recap

> *[Cold open: a simple drawn chart. A balance line goes up, and a dashed line below it follows it up.]*
>
> The rule that ends most evaluations isn't the profit target. It's this line. The one that follows you up and never comes back down.
>
> If you're in a prop-firm evaluation, or thinking about one, this is the video that matters most. Here's how trailing drawdown works, why it catches good traders, and how to size so one bad day can't end the whole thing.
>
> Every firm's rules are a little different, and they change. Check yours. I'll use round numbers so the idea is clear.
>
> **How it trails.**
>
> *[Drawing: $50,000 start, a dashed line at $47,500.]*
>
> Say it's a $50,000 account with a $2,500 trailing drawdown. On day one, your line is at $47,500. Drop below it and you're done.
>
> Now you have a good run, and the account goes up to $51,000. At a lot of firms the line moves up with you, to $48,500. Your cushion is still $2,500, but it's measured from your best point, not your starting point. *[Jack: whether the firms he knows trail at the end of the day or in real time, and that some stop trailing once you reach the starting balance plus the drawdown.]*
>
> So you can be up overall and still be one bad afternoon from failing.
>
> **Why good traders fail.**
>
> They size for the account, not for the drawdown. A $50,000 account feels big. So they take a size that would be fine on a real $50,000 account. But the number that matters isn't fifty thousand. It's twenty-five hundred.
>
> **Working backwards.**
>
> *[On screen: the free prop-firm planner.]*
>
> Start from the drawdown and ask one question: how many losing trades in a row do I want to be able to survive? I'd want at least ten. Losing streaks happen to everyone.
>
> $2,500 divided by 10 is $250 per trade. That's your risk per trade. Not what feels right, that.
>
> Now use the sizing math from the contracts video. Say the ES stop today is 6 points. On MES, the micro S&P, that's 6 times $5, so $30 a contract. $250 divided by $30 is 8.33, so 8 micros, about $240 at risk. One full ES contract on that stop would be $300, already over your limit. So this is a micros evaluation, and that's fine.
>
> **A daily stop.**
>
> The last piece: a daily loss limit, so one bad morning can't eat half the cushion. Three losing trades at $250 is $750. If your firm has its own daily limit that's tighter, use theirs. When you hit it, you're done for the day. No revenge trade. Tomorrow the evaluation is still alive.
>
> **Recap.** The line trails your best balance. Size from the drawdown, not the account. Pick a losing streak to survive, divide, then let the stop set the size. And have a daily stop.
>
> The planner on the website does this math for you, it's free and the link's below. I'm live weekdays at nine Eastern, sizing every trade this way.
>
> *[End screen: Video 11, "Passing an Evaluation Is a Sizing Problem".]*
