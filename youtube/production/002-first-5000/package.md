## 3. Full script (read once, then talk it in his own words)

**Target search:** first year trading losses · **Pillar:** Story · **Chapters:** 0:00 2008 · 2:00 What I got wrong · 4:30 Three years · 6:30 What changed · 8:30 What I'd tell myself

> *[Jack at the desk, no chart. Quieter delivery than the Lessons.]*
>
> In 2008 I opened an account with $5,000 and no idea what I was doing. I lost it. *[Jack: how long it took, in his words.]*
>
> I want to tell you how, because I don't think I was unusual. I think I was exactly what most new traders look like, and if you're in that spot right now, maybe this saves you some of it.
>
> **What I got wrong.**
>
> *[Jack: two or three real mistakes from that first account. Examples of the shape this takes: "I sized by how confident I felt", "I moved my stop when it got close", "I traded more after a loss to make it back". Only the ones that are true.]*
>
> Nobody had ever shown me how big a trade should be. I knew what a stop was. I didn't know that the stop and the size were the same decision.
>
> **The three years after.**
>
> It took me about three years of making money and losing it before I was consistent. *[Jack: what those years actually looked like. Day job? Paper trading? A specific low point?]* The thing I keep coming back to is that my entries didn't change that much. What changed was everything around them.
>
> **What changed.**
>
> *[Chart comes back on screen.]*
>
> I started deciding the dollar risk before the open, and not touching it. I started letting volatility set the stop, which is where ATR came in. And I started letting the stop decide the size, so on a wild day I'd trade smaller and on a quiet day a bit bigger. The risk stayed the same.
>
> *[Jack: the moment it clicked, if there was one.]*
>
> That was years before tastytrade, before the prop firm. Everything after was built on that.
>
> **What I'd tell myself in 2008.**
>
> You don't need a better entry yet. You need a smaller size and a stop that makes sense. Losing trades aren't the problem; big losing trades are. And nobody worth listening to needs screenshots of their winnings to convince you of anything.
>
> That's why I don't show mine. I'll show you my risk on every trade, live, every weekday at nine Eastern. That part I think can actually help.
>
> *[End screen: Video 1, "How Many Contracts Should You Trade?"]*
