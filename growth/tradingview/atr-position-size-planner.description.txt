Futures Position Size Calculator - ATR & Prop Firm Risk

DESCRIPTION (paste as-is. TradingView forbids links, websites, social handles, contact details and company names here. Keep it that way.)

A futures position size calculator for TradingView that sizes trades from risk, not from conviction. Set a risk budget, let ATR set the stop, and the panel shows the maximum number of contracts that keeps the estimated stop risk inside the budget. It supports fixed-dollar, percent-of-account and prop-firm drawdown planning, compares standard and micro contracts side by side, and draws your entry, stop and target as planning levels.

This is an open-source educational planning tool. It does not generate signals, place orders or automate trading.

THE METHOD: RISK FIRST, STOP SECOND, SIZE LAST
1. Risk budget: the most you are prepared to lose on the trade.
2. Stop: ATR x multiple from the planned entry (default 1.5 x ATR 14), rounded UP to a whole tick so it is never tighter than planned.
3. Size: maximum contracts = risk budget / (stop in points x value per point + round-turn costs per contract), rounded DOWN, so the estimated stop risk never exceeds the budget.

THE DASHBOARD
- MAX POSITION: the headline number, with the symbol, timeframe and risk mode above it.
- Risk budget, estimated stop risk, budget remaining and budget used (%).
- ATR, volatility versus its normal level, the stop in points and ticks, and the cost per contract.
- At the target (optional): the hypothetical reward NET of the same round-turn costs used in the estimated stop risk, with the gross amount and the costs shown beside it.
- Text size (Small, Normal, Large), Full or Compact layout, and four panel positions.

RISK MODES: HOW TO USE EACH
- Fixed $: enter the amount you will risk per trade.
- % of account: enter the account size and the percentage to risk (for example 50,000 at 1% = 500).
- Prop firm: enter the original maximum drawdown and the REMAINING drawdown allowance from your firm's dashboard, the losing streak you want to be able to survive (default 10) and a safety buffer (default 10%). Risk budget = remaining allowance x (1 - buffer) / losing streak. The panel shows the remaining allowance against the original and how much is used.
- Daily loss constraint (any mode, switch it on to use it): enter what is left of today's loss limit and how many full losses it must cover (default 2). Daily cap per trade = remaining daily allowance x (1 - buffer, Prop firm mode only) / planned daily losses. The risk budget is the smaller of the cap and the mode's budget. With the constraint on, an allowance of 0 means the day's limit is used up: the budget is 0 and the panel reads "Risk budget exhausted - no trade."

Prop firms define drawdown differently. A trailing limit moves up with your peak balance; a static one does not. Daily limits, reset times and other rules vary by firm. This is a planning aid. It does not check your account against any firm's rules; staying within them remains your responsibility.

STANDARD VERSUS MICRO
For ES/MES, NQ/MNQ, YM/MYM, RTY/M2K, CL/MCL, GC/MGC, SI/SIL, 6E/M6E and BTC/MBT, an optional block compares both contracts for the same stop distance. Each contract is rounded to its own tick size and uses its own value per point and its own round-turn cost (separate inputs). It shows the maximum contracts, estimated stop risk and remaining budget for each, from either the standard or the micro chart.

SUPPORTED INSTRUMENTS
Any symbol with a point value: the main size uses the chart symbol's tick size and point value (or a value you enter). The comparison block covers the nine pairs above using CME contract specifications. Other symbols show the main size without the comparison. Check all specifications against your broker's.

WORKED EXAMPLES (arithmetic only, not market data or recommendations)
- ES, $500 budget, ATR 4.00, 1.5 x ATR: stop 6.00 points (24 ticks) x $50 = $300 per contract. Max 1 contract, estimated stop risk $300, $200 of budget remaining.
- Same stop on MES ($5 per point): $30 per contract, max 16 contracts, $480 estimated stop risk.
- NQ, $500 budget, ATR 20, 1.5 x ATR: 30 points x $20 = $600 per contract, more than the budget, so 0 NQ. MNQ: 30 x $2 = $60, max 8 contracts, $480.
- MNQ with costs: 138-point stop, $2 per point, $5 round-turn cost: $281 per contract. At a 2R target the hypothetical reward is $552 gross, $547 net of costs.
- Prop firm: $2,500 remaining drawdown, 10% buffer, 10 losses: $225 budget. With the daily constraint on and $100 left today over 2 losses: $45.

PLANNING LEVELS ON THE CHART
Entry, stop and an optional target (an R multiple) are drawn as planning levels, labelled PLAN. Choose Clean (lines only), Prices, or Prices + risk (estimated stop risk at the stop and the hypothetical net reward at the target). Planning for Long or Short shades the risk and target zones. These are drawings only: no orders are placed and nothing is executed. A target reward is hypothetical and is only realised if price reaches the target and the fill matches the plan.

VOLATILITY AND ATR
- ATR is TradingView's standard ATR (Wilder's smoothing), the same as the built-in Average True Range with RMA smoothing.
- ATR timeframe: the chart's, or a higher one (for example a 15-minute ATR on a 5-minute chart). The panel warns if it is lower than the chart's.
- "Use the last closed bar's ATR" reads the previous completed bar of the ATR timeframe, so it does not look ahead and does not repaint. With it off, ATR (and any alert based on it) updates while the bar forms.
- Volatility versus normal: ATR against its own 100-bar average (adjustable). Above 1.25x is elevated, below 0.8x is quiet.

ALERTS
- Position size changed.
- Volatility expanded (ATR crossed above 1.25x its normal).

LIMITATIONS
- A planning aid, not a signal or an order. It does not tell you when, where or which way to trade.
- Slippage, gaps and fast markets can fill a stop beyond its level, so a loss can exceed the estimate. Costs are included only if you enter them.
- Amounts are in the chart symbol's currency; the panel notes when that is not USD.
- Very small budgets can round down to 0 contracts.
