The method

Risk first. Stop second. Size last.

Most traders pick a size, then squeeze the stop to fit it. The planner works the other way round.

  1. STEP 01

    Choose your dollar risk

    The most you're willing to lose if the trade is wrong. It's the only number you choose.

  2. STEP 02

    Let ATR set the stop

    The stop sits a multiple of ATR from price (default 1.5 × ATR 14), rounded up to a whole tick so it's never tighter than planned.

  3. STEP 03

    Let the stop set the size

    Contracts = risk ÷ (stop in points × dollars per point), rounded down so the real risk never exceeds your limit.

Worked examples

Same risk. Different markets.

Illustrations of the arithmetic, not live market data or a trade recommendation.

ES with $500 of risk. ATR(14) reads 4.00 points. 1.5 × ATR = a 6.00-point stop (24 ticks). ES is $50 a point, so one contract risks $300. $500 ÷ $300 = 1.66, rounded down to 1 contract, with $300 actual risk.

NQ with $500 of risk. ATR(14) reads 20 points, so the stop is 30 points. NQ is $20 a point: $600 for one contract. That's more than $500, so the planner shows 0: skip the trade or use the micro.

MNQ, same stop. MNQ is $2 a point: $60 per contract. $500 ÷ $60 = 8.3, rounded down to 8 contracts, with $480 actual risk.

On your chart

What the planner shows.

  • Maximum position size, firstThe headline number, with the symbol, timeframe and risk mode above it. Then risk budget, estimated stop risk, budget remaining and budget used.
  • Standard vs micro, side by sideES/MES, NQ/MNQ, YM/MYM, RTY/M2K, CL/MCL, GC/MGC, SI/SIL, 6E/M6E and BTC/MBT, for the same stop, each with its own tick size, value per point and costs.
  • Prop-firm planningRemaining drawdown against the original, a safety buffer and the losing streak to survive. An optional daily loss constraint spreads what's left of today's limit over your planned daily losses (two by default). When it's used up, the panel says no trade.
  • Volatility vs normalATR compared with its own 100-bar average: quiet, normal or elevated. It shows why your size changed today.
  • Planning levelsEntry, stop and an R-multiple target, as clean lines or with prices and estimated risk. Shaded zones when you plan one side. Drawings only: no orders are placed.
  • Your settings, your wayAdjustable text size, compact or full panel, costs per contract, higher-timeframe or closed-bar ATR, and alerts when the size changes or volatility expands.

Install

Add it in under a minute.

A free TradingView account is enough. Works on the web, desktop and mobile apps.

  1. 01

    Open a futures chart

    For example ES1!, NQ1!, MES1! or CL1! on TradingView.

  2. 02

    Find the script

    Click Indicators at the top of the chart, search for Futures Position Size Calculator - ATR & Prop Firm Risk, and click it to add it. Star it to keep it in Favorites.

  3. 03

    Set your numbers

    Open the indicator's settings (gear icon): risk per trade, ATR length and stop multiple. Leave "Dollars per point" at 0 to use the symbol's own value.

Know the limits

What it doesn't do.

It isn't a signal

The planner sizes a trade you've already decided to consider. It doesn't say when, where or which way to trade.

ATR changes while a bar is forming

On the live bar, ATR (and so the stop and size) can change until the bar closes. Turn on "Use the last closed bar's ATR" for numbers that stay fixed. You can also take ATR from a higher timeframe than the chart.

The point value depends on the symbol

It uses TradingView's point value for the chart symbol. For non-futures symbols, CFDs or anything unusual, type the dollars per point in yourself and check it against your broker's contract specs.

Costs and slippage

Commissions and fees are included only if you enter them (separately for standard and micro contracts). Slippage isn't: gaps and fast markets can fill a stop well beyond its level, so the real loss can exceed the estimated stop risk.

Planning levels are not orders

The entry, stop and target lines are drawings to plan with. Nothing is sent to a broker. The reward shown at a target is not a forecast or an expected return: it's only realised if price gets there and your fill matches the plan. It's shown net of the same round-turn costs used in the estimated stop risk, with the gross amount beside it.

Prop-firm rules vary

Firms define drawdown differently: a trailing limit moves up with your peak balance, a static one doesn't. Enter the current remaining allowances from your firm's dashboard. With the daily constraint switched on, the cap per trade is the remaining daily allowance, less the safety buffer, divided by your planned daily losses; an allowance of $0 means a budget of $0. The planner is a planning aid: it doesn't check your account against any firm's rules, so staying within them remains your responsibility.

Rounding

Stops round up to a whole tick and contracts round down, which keeps actual risk at or below your limit. Very small risk amounts can therefore show 0.
